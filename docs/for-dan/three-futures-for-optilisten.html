Three futures for OptiListen

Apple is going to remove OptiListen from the App Store on 24 November unless we update it. That deadline made me go back and look at the whole thing properly — what it was, what changed around it, and what it could still be. Here's all of it, including a rebuilt version that already runs.

Where we started

The version you know measured how much you talked

You set the phone beside you on a call and afterward it told you what share of the talking was yours. In 2021 that was a real thing to know and nothing else would tell you.

Two things have happened since, and they point in opposite directions.

The thing we built is now a free feature of everything.

Talk-time measurement got absorbed. Zoom, Teams, Granola, Gong and Fathom all report who spoke how much, at higher accuracy than a phone on a table can manage, without anyone remembering to start anything. Rebuilding OptiListen as a measurement app in 2026 means competing with a checkbox inside tools people already have open.

And it never really worked the way it promised. I went back through the old build to be sure about this. On an iPhone, an app that isn't playing audio gets suspended shortly after you switch away from it — so the original app stopped listening roughly thirty seconds into a call and reported a confident percentage based on that. The team raised it to fifty seconds; Apple rejected a build over the workaround in July 2023. The number people saw was a sample of the first minute, presented as the meeting.

What nobody is doing

Every one of those tools tells you what happened. None of them helps you get better.

A transcript summary arrives after the meeting, when there's nothing left to do about it. That's analytics. What no tool does is treat a conversation as something you practice — which is the only framing under which any of this changes how someone actually behaves.

Before

Commit to something

Set a ceiling and write one line: "ask before answering." Ten seconds, and now there's something to fail at.

During

See the gap while it matters

One number and your own line, on the table in front of you, at the moment you can still do something about it.

After

Say how present you were

Not what the microphone heard — what you know. You can hit your number and still have been somewhere else entirely.

The unit stops being the number and becomes the loop. In the rebuild, a conversation counts as practiced if you set an intention and reflected on it — even if there's no measurement at all. A perfect ratio with no reflection doesn't count. That inversion is the whole product argument, and it's the one thing none of the incumbents can copy without changing what they are.

Honest status

What's real, and what isn't yet

Working
  • The full loop, before → during → after, end to end.
  • Live listening. Loudness only, on the phone, in the moment — no recording, no transcript, no account, nothing sent anywhere.
  • A calibration step that learns your voice against the room, and that will tell you honestly when it can't — rather than inventing a number.
  • Typing the number yourself instead, so the loop still closes with headphones on or the phone in a pocket.
  • It's uploaded and in Apple's test system right now.
Not yet
  • It crashes. I hit it on my own phone and we have not found it yet — we've fixed two plausible causes and neither was the one. That's the current work, and it's why I haven't put it on your phone.
  • No first-run walkthrough. It drops you straight in.
  • Calibration forgets itself between launches.
  • Reading the number out of Zoom or Granola is designed for but not built — their access is locked behind business plans today.
  • New store screenshots still to make.

I find the third number more interesting than the first two. Nobody has touched this app in three years, and people are still searching their way to it and installing it. That's not a business — it's five people a month — but it does say the phrase "listen better" sends people looking, and that the thing they found gave them nothing to come back to. Which is exactly what a measurement-only app would do.

Three futures

Laid out evenly, because I don't think this one is mine to decide

These are genuinely different bets, not a ladder with a right answer at the top. I've tried to write each one as its own advocate would.

Keep the shelf space

Ship the smallest honest update that satisfies Apple, and leave OptiListen exactly what it is. Decide the rest later, with more information and no deadline.

What it means
The rebuilt app goes up because it has to be modern to be accepted at all, but we don't promote it, don't extend it, and don't ask anything of it.
What it takes
Finishing the crash and the store paperwork. Weeks, not months, and most of it is already done.
What it wins
The listing stays alive, the name stays ours, and the five-people-a-month keeps trickling. Every other option stays open indefinitely.
What it costs
Nothing changes. It stays a measurement app in a world that measures for free, and we'll be having this same conversation whenever we next look up.

Build the practice tool

Treat the rebuild as the beginning rather than the compliance step, and go make the thing none of the meeting tools are going to make.

What it means
Finish the loop properly — the walkthrough, the reflection you come back to, a reason to open it a second time — and put it in front of people who already want to be better at this: coaches, facilitators, new managers.
What it takes
Sustained attention over months, not a push. Design, real people testing it, and someone caring whether anyone comes back.
What it wins
A claim nobody else is making, in a category everyone else has reduced to a statistic. If practice-as-a-product works at all, it works here.
What it costs
The time, and a real risk: practicing is a habit, and most people don't want one. Those 96 downloads are not evidence that they do.

Retire it on purpose

Let it go deliberately, while we can still say what it was for, rather than letting Apple's sweep do it for us in November.

What it means
No update. On 24 November the listing comes down. We keep optilisten.com, write the honest account of what we tried and what the market did, and put it up there.
What it takes
An afternoon. Everything else is already built.
What it wins
A clean ending and the attention back. OptiListen was three years early to something that is now standard equipment, and that's a real thing to have been.
What it costs
The listing and the name, permanently. Starting again later means a new record, a new identity, and none of the history.
