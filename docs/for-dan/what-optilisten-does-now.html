Where we started

The version you know measured how much you talked

You put the phone beside you on a call, and it told you what share of the conversation was your voice. Simple, and in 2021 nothing else did it.

Two things have happened since. Apple has told me the app will be pulled from the store on November 24 unless we ship an update — the old code is three years stale and can't be updated as-is, so an update means a rebuild. And in the meantime, every meeting tool grew that same feature. Zoom, Teams, Google Meet, the note-taking apps — they all show you your talk time now, for free, without you doing anything.

So rebuilding the measuring part alone would put us back on the shelf next to products that do it better, built in, for nothing. That's the part of the situation that shaped everything below.

The idea

Nobody helps you practice listening. That's the gap.

All those tools measure you. None of them ask you to decide anything before a conversation, none nudge you while it's still happening, and none ask afterward whether you were actually there. Your original screens did all three — the goal, the live number, the "how present were you" question — and that was the good part all along.

So the rebuild makes those three moments the whole product:

Before
Set an intention

How much of this conversation do you mean to spend talking? And in one line: what are you practicing? "Ask before answering." "Let silences run."

During
See the gap while it counts

Your share of the talking, live, next to the ceiling you set — and the line you wrote, right there on the screen, while you can still act on it.

After
Say how it went

How present were you, one to five? What happened? Your own read, not the microphone's. This is the part that makes it practice instead of a report.

The count on the home screen isn't your average talk share. It's how many conversations you set an intention for and came back to. A conversation with a number but no reflection doesn't count. One with a reflection and no number does.

Honest status

What works, what doesn't, and why the test build crashes

Built and working

  • All four screens, front to back
  • The intention → listening → reflection loop, saved on the phone
  • Live speaking share from the microphone, with a one-time setup that learns your voice against the room and refuses to give a number if the room is too noisy to trust
  • The headphones mode (no number, just your line)
  • Nothing leaves the phone. No account, no server, no recording.

Not there yet

  • The build on TestFlight right now crashes the moment you tap Start. A required one-line permission notice was left out of that build. Fixed; a new build is on its way. Don't judge it by this one.
  • The one-time voice setup doesn't remember itself between launches yet
  • The "Still open" list on Home shows, but tapping through to finish one isn't wired up
  • No app icon polish, no onboarding, no App Store screenshots

Two things I want to be straight about. First, the microphone approach is the same idea the 2021 app used — the phone sits beside you and listens to the room — but the old app only managed about a minute before the phone shut it off in the background. That's the thing that quietly broke it for everyone. The new one doesn't fight that: it's meant to sit on the desk with the screen on, like a timer. Second, it's never been used in a real conversation yet. It compiles clean and runs; whether it feels right is exactly what I need you for.

The decision

Three ways this can go

1
Keep the shelf space

Ship the rebuild as-is to satisfy Apple, keep the listing alive, and leave it there. Costs almost nothing more than we've already spent. Doesn't ask anything of anyone.

2
Make it the practice thing

Commit to the before-during-after idea above, get it in front of people who'd actually use it — coaches, facilitators, anyone who runs meetings for a living — and see if it holds. More work, and the first real question is whether anyone comes back on day two.

3
Let it go, well

Let Apple pull it, or pull it ourselves, and write the honest story: a 2021 app that named a real problem before the tools caught up. Not a failure. Some things are precursors.

I'll have a recommendation once you've held the working build and told me what you think. I'm not attached to any of the three, and I don't want to decide this without you.

What I'm asking

Two things, neither urgent this week

React to the idea. Does before-during-after feel like what OptiListen was always trying to be, or does it feel like a different app wearing its name? Both are useful answers.

Wait for the next build, then try it on one real call. I'll tell you when it's up. Phone on the desk, screen on, one intention. Then tell me whether the "how present were you" question landed or felt silly. That single reaction is worth more than anything else I could measure.
