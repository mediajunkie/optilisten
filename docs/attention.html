Standing risks

▲
Two builds shipped against two theories, and nothing diagnosed. Both theories were real defects; neither was the one. The pattern to break is reasoning from source to a mechanism we can see when the reporter has already named a mechanism we'd have to go look for. Pard's own rule, adopted here: when a report names two things and you can only explain one, the one you can't explain is the finding.
▲
No instrumentation. Every crash this month was found because a human installed a build and hit it. There is no crash reporting in the app and no alert when a submission lands. xian raised this on 09-14 and 09-14 is the argument for it.
▲
Compiling is not running, and running is not being used. 2.0 has been launched on a real phone twice and died both times. Nobody has watched calibration, the mic tap, or the loop behave in an actual conversation.
▲
Calibration is Codable and nothing persists it — recalibrates on every cold launch.
▲
Apple rejected this app once before over background modes (July 2023). 2.0 omits UIBackgroundModes deliberately — which is also, possibly, the thing making it crash. If the fix needs an audio background mode, that is a submission-risk conversation, not just a code change.
▲
The 24 Nov date is still the screenshot. The mail forward works; nobody has looked. Two accepted builds may well have moved it.
▲
One signing path, expiring Aug 2027. The API can renew it; nothing watches for expiry. Age-rating questions still due at submission, ~10 min, answers are "no".

Method notes

the week's, condensed
When a report names two things and you can only find a mechanism for one, the one you can't explain is the finding. xian's first sentence on 09-11 was "crashes as soon as I fill out the first screen and it tries to go to background." Pard quoted that sentence in his own HOLD memo and then reasoned only about its first half — because calibration-then-tap was a mechanism he could see and backgrounding was one he'd have had to go looking for. I relayed both halves and then did the same thing. Three days.
Going from one hypothesis to zero is a worse position than it looks. Janus's framing, and the reason no third build ships on a story. The obvious move after a refutation is to generate another plausible mechanism and ship against it, which feels like progress and is how the last three days were spent.
The channel where the property lives, not the channel you're used to. The crash submissions were one authenticated GET away for three days while three agents traded inferences. Same class of error as the mailbox conventions.
Build identity is settled by the vendor's timestamps, not by anyone's recollection. Janus resolved "which build did he run" from Apple's processing mail and the count of such mails — a question that would otherwise have cost another round trip through a human who was heads-down elsewhere.
Reading the artifact means diffing it against the full intent, not spot-checking the fields that burned you last time. Pard verified version, bundle and signing out of the built archive and called it verified; the validator then found four absent keys in the same file. His words, and they apply to me at least as much.
The instruction is not the artifact. A 2022 email said com.optilisten.ios; the project file that shipped said com.longskymedia.optilisten. Five days in a build config before anyone opened the file that mattered.
Three agreeing checks from one vantage point are one check. "No accounts," "no keychain item," "empty plist" all read the same session. The account was there. A headless session on Amber reports "not visible from here," never "absent."
Two people can both be right if they're reading different stores. The reflex is to work out who's mistaken. It cost a day. The answer was a keychain the SSH session couldn't open.
Written for you, read by him. The update note to Dan was accurate and unreadable. The audience for a page decides its vocabulary, not the author's week.
